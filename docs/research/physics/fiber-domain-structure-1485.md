# Crystallite structure of fiber CXR: stacking, mosaic and domains

**Status:** Phase II research note for [#1485](https://github.com/Quantum-Light-Matter-Cooperative-QLMC/pyrite/issues/1485), part of [#1480](https://github.com/Quantum-Light-Matter-Cooperative-QLMC/pyrite/issues/1480). It changes no production default and no catalog entry. Calculations: `checks/fiber_domain_structure_1485.py`. The baseline is the ideal Bernal fiber average of the [#1480 note](fiber-nonbasal-cxr-1480.md). That note's [Domain coherence (open)](fiber-nonbasal-cxr-1480.md#domain-coherence-open) section lists the three effects measured here.

**Question.** The #1480 fiber average assumes three things: Bernal (AB) crystallites, one roll per coherent region, and no mosaic. How much do turbostratic stacking, mosaic tilt combined with the roll, and phase breaks between crystallites in the coherent mode change the yields of the #1480 scenes? Which domain model should each catalog fiber crystal use?

**Short answer.**

- **Turbostratic stacking** changes the result materially, and the change is spectral more than integrated. Fully turbostratic rods ($p=1$) change the integrated CXR line yield by +0.9 % to +37 % against the ideal Bernal fiber. The spectral L1 against Bernal at 5 % energy resolution is 0.21–1.06. The rods also remove most of the non-basal intensity that #1480 found inside the (00l) peak window: full over basal in a ±2 % window drops from 1.10–1.51 (Bernal) to 1.01–1.12 (rods).
- **Mosaic composed with the roll** at the HOPG spreads of 0.4–0.8° changes integrated yields by at most 0.1 %, with line-shape L1 of 1–17 % at 5 % resolution. A 3.5° spread adds up to +6.6 %. Pyrolytic-BN spreads matter more. At 5° (HOPBN grade) h-BN yields rise by +0.3 % to +10 %, and at 30° (as-deposited PBN) by +6 % to +48 %. That is comparable to the rod effect.
- **Domain phases in the coherent mode** change the coherent yield by at most 1.8 %, with smoothed L1 at most 3.6 %, for every crystallite cell from 1 µm × 30 nm to 10 µm × 300 nm.

**Proposal:** HOPG keeps the ideal Bernal fiber with mosaic composed through the existing tilt quadrature. Fiber averaging applies to HOPG only. Catalog `hbn` is one oriented single crystal (user decisions, 2026-11-27): a millimetre HPHT flake with $c$ along the surface normal, ordered 2H (AA′) stacking, $d_{002}\approx3.33$ Å and a mosaic of about 0.025°. Its in-plane azimuth is unknown. The roll-averaged fiber model does not apply to it. The pyrolytic-BN rod and broad-mosaic results stay as recorded measurements and no longer drive the catalog proposal. Neither crystal needs a coherent-mode domain model; coherent full-fiber emission stays refused (see [Proposed domain model](#proposed-domain-model-per-catalog-crystal)).

## Literature

Every source below is in Zotero and was read from its indexed full text (OCR for the scans). Equation numbers follow the papers.

- **Warren, "X-ray diffraction in random layer lattices", Phys. Rev. 59, 693 (1941)** (`VT5X3KDG`). Layers are parallel and equidistant, with random in-plane translations $\delta,\epsilon$ (Eq. 1). A random rotation about the normal changes nothing further in a powder pattern (Sect. II). For $h=k=0$ the sum is crystalline and independent of the randomness (Eq. 6). For $(hk)\neq0$ the phases between layers are random, so the layers scatter incoherently and the intensity is that of one layer times their number (Eq. 7). The reciprocal lattice becomes lines through $H=hb_1+kb_2$ normal to the layer, with $s-s_0=H+p_3b_3$ and $p_3$ continuous (Eq. 8, Fig. 1). This is the $p=1$ limit of the stacking model, $I=C_0$ per layer along the whole rod, with $(00l)$ unchanged. Warren also gives the $(hk)$ powder-band shape, the layer size from its breadth, $L=1.84\lambda/(B\cos\theta)$ (Eq. 41), the peak shift toward larger angle (Sect. V) and the integrated intensity (Eqs. 43–46). These are powder-profile results that the fiber model does not use. His integrated intensity per $(hk)$ band is $F^2$ per layer times the intercepted length of the rod (Eq. 44), which is the same normalization as the Parseval statement below. (Equation numbers 6–8 are read from the OCR order and should be confirmed on the page scan.)
- **Hendricks and Teller, "X-ray interference in partially ordered layer lattices", J. Chem. Phys. 10, 147 (1942)** (`XMSKDM9C`). The intensity per layer is the sum over all layer pairs of $\lvert V_k\rvert\lvert V_0\rvert\cos(\varphi_k-\varphi_0)$ (Eq. 1). For independent phase shifts the average of each pair term factorizes by complete induction (Eqs. 3–5), and the geometric sum gives the closed form (Eq. 6), $I=V^2(1-r^2)/(1-2r\cos\psi+r^2)$ with $r=\cos\tfrac12(\varphi^{(1)}-\varphi^{(2)})$. Section 4 generalizes this to several layer kinds with probabilities and phases (Eq. 14), again by induction over intermediate layers (Eqs. 15–19), and sums it geometrically (Eq. 20, denominator $1-2C\cos\psi+C^2$). The model here is a special case of Section 4: two layer kinds, A and B, that alternate deterministically between faults, and a fault that sets the in-plane correlation to zero. The per-interface factor is then $1-p$. With one layer kind ($F_A=F_B$), $I_p$ reduces exactly to their Eq. (6) with $r=1-p$, which is a Poisson kernel of mean 1 (the Parseval check). The final section treats graphite (and close-packed) stacking with second-neighbour irregularities, AB against ABC. That is the rhombohedral fault family, which this note does not model. The paper excludes finite size, thermal motion and beam divergence (Sect. 1, p. 149). The note adds thermal factors through the production Debye–Waller term and leaves the other two out.
- **Ohler et al., "X-ray topographic determination of the granular structure in a graphite mosaic crystal", J. Appl. Cryst. 33, 1023 (2000)** (`BR5JJNP6`). This is section topography of the 004 reflection of HOPG. The crystal is made of crystalline domains up to several hundred micrometres along the basal planes (conclusion i). Inside a domain the material is still mosaic, about 0.15° FWHM (voxel fits 0.15 ± 0.02°; area means 0.13–0.16°, Fig. 9). That is about 60 % of the sample's global spread, so the sample is about 0.25°, and the misorientation between domains accounts for the other 40 % (conclusions ii–iii). HOPG mosaic is "typically in the range 0.1–3°" (introduction). The voxels are about 118 × 23 × 7 µm, so the paper resolves domains, not coherent crystallites. It gives no $L_a$ or $L_c$ of the coherently scattering crystallites.

Citing the topography work: crystallites "some µm in size" arranged into mosaic blocks about 100 µm in diameter and about 10 µm thick, with the spread between crystallites much smaller than that between blocks. ## Stacking model

The layers keep the Bernal spacing $d=c/2$. Each interface is a turbostratic fault with probability $p$: a random rotation about $c$ together with a random in-plane translation. With probability $1-p$ the Bernal A/B alternation continues. Layer A holds the basis atoms at $z=0$ and layer B those at $z=\tfrac12$. In-plane, their form factors are $F_A$ and $F_B$, including the in-plane phases $e^{2\pi i(hx+ky)}$.

A fault removes every in-plane ($hk\neq0$) correlation across it. A rotation moves the next layer's rods to another azimuth, and a translation randomizes their phase. The correlation of two layers $n$ apart is therefore $(1-p)^{\lvert n\rvert}C_n$, with $C_\text{even}=\tfrac12(\lvert F_A\rvert^2+\lvert F_B\rvert^2)\equiv C_0$ and $C_\text{odd}=\operatorname{Re}F_AF_B^*$ (averaged over the starting layer). The intensity per layer along the $(hk)$ rod, at $x=g_zd$, is

$$
I_p(x)=C_0\,[1+2S_\text{even}]+2\operatorname{Re}(F_AF_B^*)\,S_\text{odd},
\qquad
S_\text{all}=\operatorname{Re}\frac{z}{1-z},\quad
S_\text{even}=\operatorname{Re}\frac{z^2}{1-z^2},\quad
S_\text{odd}=S_\text{all}-S_\text{even},
$$

with $z=(1-p)e^{ix}$. This is the Hendricks–Teller form for a two-layer Markov sequence whose fault resets the correlation. The `profile` subcommand checks three limits:

- **$p=1$** (fully turbostratic, Warren's random layer lattice): $I=C_0$ for every $x$. The $(hk)$ reflections become rods with $g_z$ continuous, and no general $(hkl)$ points remain.
- **$p\to0$:** $I_p$ collapses onto the Bernal points. $(2/\pi)\int I\,dx$ over a peak equals the Bernal $\lvert S\rvert^2$ of that point, $\lvert F_A\pm F_B\rvert^2$ for even and odd $l$. At $p=0.002$ this holds to 0.1 % for graphite and h-BN, $(10)$, $(11)$ and $(20)$.
- **Every $p$ (Parseval):** the mean of $I_p$ over one period is $C_0$. Stacking disorder redistributes intensity along the rod and conserves it.

For graphite with $m=h-k\not\equiv0\pmod 3$, $\lvert F_A\rvert^2=\lvert F_B\rvert^2$ and $\operatorname{Re}F_AF_B^*=-\tfrac12\lvert F_A\rvert^2$, so the Bernal ratio of odd to even $l$ is 3:1. For $m\equiv0$ it is 0:4 (odd $l$ extinct). h-BN's odd $l$ survive only through $f_B-f_N$ (see the [#1480 structure factors](fiber-nonbasal-cxr-1480.md#structure-factors)). Basal $(00l)$ rows are unaffected by $p$ ($g_\perp=0$).

**Assumptions not modelled:** turbostratic interlayer expansion moves every line, basal included, by $-\Delta d/d$. For carbon that is 3.42–3.44 Å against 3.354 Å, about −2 to −2.5 %. For pyrolytic BN it is 3.40–3.46 Å (Type I) or 3.34–3.39 Å (Type II) against 3.33 Å, so −0.3 to −3.9 % (Moore 1990). Both arms of every comparison use the catalog $c$, so the expansion shifts rods and Bernal points together. It enters the rod-versus-Bernal deltas only through the second-order change of $\chi_g$ and of the resonance energies, and it does not change the decision. Layer bending, finite layer size $L_a$ along the rod ($\Delta g_\perp\sim2\pi/L_a$) and interlayer-spacing fluctuations are also left out. Rhombohedral (ABC) stacking in HOPG is not modelled; it is a different ordered sequence, not a fault in this model.

**Scoring rods through the production kernel.** A one-layer hexagonal cell (layer A of the catalog basis, $c=d$, $V=V_\text{Bernal}/2$) gives each rod node $(h,k,l')$ the exact layer amplitude $F_A(g)$ with production Henke form factors, recoil, polarization, absorption and PXR/CBS couplings. The node weight $w=\Delta l'\,I_p/\lvert F_A\rvert^2$ (form factor $f_0$ in the ratio) is folded into the scalar Debye–Waller argument, $B\to B-8\pi^2\ln w/g^2$. That multiplies both couplings by $\sqrt w$ exactly (checked to machine precision against a direct rescale). With one layer per cell, the rod per unit $g_z$ carries the same intensity as the Bernal points per unit $g_z$. So the Bernal discrete sum and the rod integral are the same Parseval mean, and only its distribution along $g_z$ differs. Node positions $l'=(j+u)\Delta l'$ use a stratified jitter $u$ per electron stratum. The comb is then an unbiased estimate of the continuous rod.

- The production coupling tables look a callable $B$ up by the integer cast of the Friedel mate. Nodes $j+u$ and $j-1+u$ near $l'=0$ collide there, so the check scores them in separate calls.
- Graphite uses the #535 tensor $B_\text{eff}(g)$ on rods as in #1480, and h-BN the catalog $B_{33}$ isotropically (the #1480 h-BN caveat, #1482).
- **Single-flight check** (30 keV, 500 nm, tilt 15°, 45°, rod $(10)$ only, 8 sector rolls). The fine rod at $p=0.05$, $\Delta l'=1/512$, reproduces the Bernal $(10l)$ family sum to −0.3 % (HOPG). For h-BN it gives +3.5 %, where the $f_0$-based weight misjudges the weak $f_B-f_N$ odd-$l$ peaks. The $p=1$ rod changes the $(10)$ yield by +4.9 % (HOPG) and −10 % (h-BN).

- **Where the intensity goes.** The rods spread each Bernal $(hkl)$ line along continuous $g_z$. Yield moves out of the (00l) windows into broad bands, mostly below the (002) line. For h-BN at 300 keV, the band 300 eV to 0.45 $E_{002}$ holds 0.4 % of the Bernal yield and 24 % (relative to the Bernal total) under rods; the ±10 % band around (002) falls from 85 % to 56 %. HOPG at 300 keV shows the same pattern: 1.4 % → 22 %, and 58 % → 41 %. The low-$l'$ part of each rod, where Bernal has only the $(hk0)$ points, radiates strongly at oblique incidence. That is why the scenes with large tilt or high energy change most.
- **Integrated yield is not Parseval-conserved.** Parseval conserves the mean $\lvert S\rvert^2$ per unit $g_z$, but the emission weight per unit $\lvert\chi_g\rvert^2$ varies along the rod: resonance energy, $v\cdot g>0$, polarization and absorption. The integrated change therefore depends on the scene. In the 30 keV HOPG scene the line shapes change (L1 0.21) while the total stays within 1 %.
- **Intermediate $p$** interpolates monotonically in ΔY and L1 (for example h-BN 300 keV: L1 0.48, 0.83, 1.06 at $p$ = 0.2, 0.5, 1). A Bernal/turbostratic mixture of fraction $f$ gives $f$ times the $p=1$ column, to first order.
- **Reciprocal cutoff.** At 8 Å⁻¹ (30 keV, tilt 15°, lab 45°, stacking arms only) the $p=1$ change is +0.5 % for HOPG (+0.9 % at 6 Å⁻¹) and +3.0 % for h-BN (+7.7 % at 6 Å⁻¹). L1 at 5 % stays at 0.20 and 0.31. The spectral redistribution is cutoff-stable, but the integrated rod-versus-Bernal change is not converged at 6 Å⁻¹; #1480 found the full sum converging only near 24 Å⁻¹.
- **Cost.** Rods at $\Delta l'=1/16$ have about 1000 nodes against 126 Bernal non-basal rows at 6 Å⁻¹ (2300–2400 at 8 Å⁻¹). They cost 13–22× the Bernal arm, and the cost doubles with each halving of $\Delta l'$. Wall time for a whole scene with all arms was 7–39 min on 8 CPU threads (FP64), peak RSS about 1.1 GB.

The replica structure of the fixed-node rule is resolved by the narrow lines, as `_mosaic_sampled.py` documents. The production `FiberCXRModel` currently refuses `mosaic_sampling="history"`. Allowing it, with a seed per roll stratum or per roll node, is the cheap route to resolved mosaic line shapes.

## Coherent-mode domain phases

The coherent route (`coherent=True`, node quadrature) is run on the first 300 histories of each scene, with the inter-electron form factor forced to zero: random arrival times, a finite footprint and a 1 ps Gaussian bunch, so $F_z=0$ exactly. The coherent sum then runs within one electron. This was checked to $10^{-16}$ against one call per electron. Arms:

- `coh_ref_incoherent`: incoherent, node quadrature, same rolls;
- `coh_electron`: today's per-electron coherent sum, one crystallite per track;
- `coh_dom_La:Lc`: each track cut into crystallite cells of $L_a\times L_a\times L_c$ (random cell origin), with each (electron, cell) piece a separate emitter. Non-basal fields of different crystallites never interfere, because they have different $g$. Basal fields lose phase across a cell boundary (random stacking registry, $\chi=0$). Pieces keep the parent electron's roll, so the arms differ only in phase breaks; independent rolls per piece leave the mean unchanged (it is linear in the orientation distribution).

The columns give the coherent excess (`coh_electron` over incoherent, total and basal), and for the domain arms ΔY and smoothed L1 (50 eV boxcar) against `coh_electron`.

- **Small at the 0.1 µm scale, and nearly all basal.** In these thin, multiply-scattered scenes the intra-electron coherent excess is at most 1.8 %. Cutting tracks at any of the scanned crystallite sizes removes most of the basal part of it (to within 0–20 %). Total yield moves by at most 1.5 % and smoothed shapes by at most 2 %; the basal-only L1 is at most 3.6 %.
- **Non-basal coherence across crystallites** changes yields by at most 0.2 %. The per-electron coherent excess of non-basal rows is itself at most 0.4 %.
- **Formation length.** The result does not depend on $L_c$ between 30 and 300 nm. Every scanned size already exceeds the formation lengths of keV lines, and the excess that survives at 10 µm × 300 nm is within 0.2 % of the smaller cells. A thicker, straighter track (higher energy, thicker target) could raise the coherent excess; these scenes do not test that. ## Proposed domain model per catalog crystal

**Decision (user, 2026-11-27).** Accepted as below, with one change for HOPG: mosaic is a named commercial grade, not one fixed value. The catalog default is the 0.8° grade (`hopg.toml`), and users can select the other commercially available grades (for example ZYA, ZYB and ZYH, and HAPG). No production or catalog change is made here.

- **HOPG.** A turbostratic fraction $f$ of a few per cent changes HOPG results by about $f$ times the $p=1$ column: below 0.6 % in integrated yield and below 0.05 in L1 at 5 %, for $f\le5$ %. That is smaller than the cutoff and thermal-tensor uncertainties in the #1480 note, so a rod model is not warranted for HOPG. Rhombohedral stacking is a separate ordered polytype and is not covered by this bound. HOPG-grade mosaic leaves integrated yields unchanged within 0.1 %, so it matters only for line shapes.
- **Pyrolytic h-BN (recorded, not driving the catalog).** These results describe pyrolytic BN, which catalog `hbn` no longer represents. They remain valid measurements for any future pyrolytic-BN material entry. The rods change every measured h-BN scene beyond any other #1480 or #1485 uncertainty: integrated yield +8 % to +37 %, basal share 0.51 → 0.38 at 300 keV, L1 0.32–1.06. The extra yield in the (00l) window collapses from 14–51 % to 1–10 %. The Bernal non-basal numbers in the #1480 note therefore do not describe pyrolytic h-BN. A narrow (002)/(004) window is closer to basal-only under rods than under Bernal, but integrated and off-peak yields are not.
- **HOPG, measured inputs.** Ohler et al. (2000) give 0.15° inside a domain and about 0.25° for their sample, and HAPG reaches 0.04–0.06°. The 0.4–0.8° arms are therefore upper bounds for good HOPG, and the "line shape only" conclusion holds. Crystallites of a few µm and blocks of about 100 × 10 µm sit at or above the largest coherent cell scanned, so the coherent-mode conclusion holds too.
- **Partial order: what the pyrolytic-BN literature says** (context for a future pyrolytic-BN entry). Pyrolytic BN is not one material. Moore (1990) classes low-deposition-temperature PBN (about 1700 °C) as Type I, purely turbostratic, $d_{002}>3.40$ Å, "like that of pyrolytic carbon". That is $p=1$. Higher-temperature deposits (Type II, about 1900–2000 °C, 3.34–3.39 Å) are "more crystalline", which is partial order, $p<1$. Commercial PBN is usually a Type III + I or Type III + II mixture, and Type III adds a columnar, twinned component with rocking-curve peaks 70–75° apart. Moore et al. (1989) find as-deposited material turbostratic (3.40 Å) and reach three-dimensional order only by compression annealing at 2500–2700 °C. Alkoy et al. (1997) see (102) appear, a sign of three-dimensional order, only after 1500–1800 °C anneals of turbostratic powder. Paszkowicz et al. (2002) show that h-BN powders span $P_3$ = 0.23–0.98 at nearly the ideal $c$, so $d_{002}$ alone does not fix $p$. No source in the library measures $p$ or an $(hk)$ rod profile for pyrolytic BN. The literature therefore supports $p=1$ for as-deposited Type I PBN, partial order for Type II or high-temperature grades, and Bernal-like order only for annealed HOPBN. If a measured rod profile, or a $(10l)$ intensity ratio, becomes available, $p$ is the single parameter to fit, and its effect interpolates monotonically (table above).
- **Catalog h-BN is an oriented single crystal (resolved 2026-11-27, user).** The sample is one large-size 2D Semiconductors flake, a few mm × a few mm, and the electron beam is of similar size. It is mounted with the surface parallel to (00l), so $c$ lies along the surface normal. The user did not state that the in-plane azimuth is controlled, so it is treated as unknown unless it is measured. One flake under a beam of its own size gives one fixed roll, not the #1480 roll average, so **the fiber model does not apply to catalog `hbn`**. The right model is the oriented single crystal: `surface_hkl` (0,0,1), ordered 2H stacking, $d_{002}\approx3.33$ Å, mosaic about 0.025°. Fiber averaging stays appropriate for HOPG only.
  - **Basal (00l) lines** do not depend on the azimuth. Their fiber and single-crystal results agree, and they need only the catalog $B_{33}$.
  - **Non-basal lines** depend on the in-plane azimuth: which $(hkl)$ reach $v\cdot g>0$, their resonance energies and their polarization all move with it. There are two ways forward. Measure the azimuth of the mounted flake (for example by Laue) and model that orientation, or treat the azimuth as a parameter to scan, or marginalize over it when it cannot be measured. A uniform marginal over azimuth reproduces the fiber average, but a single measurement is one draw from it, not its mean.
  - **In-plane ADPs (#1482)** become a prerequisite for every non-basal h-BN line. The catalog $B_{33}$ is basal-only.
  - **Follow-up (catalog change, not done here):** `hbn.toml` still carries `texture = "fiber"` with the comment "pyrolytic/fiber-textured BN", which no longer describes the decided material.
  - The fiber-model h-BN numbers in this note and in #1480 (Bernal and rod arms) therefore describe a multi-crystal or pyrolytic sample, not catalog `hbn`. Its basal shares are still the azimuth-independent part.
- **Stacking for ordered h-BN.** No stacking-fault measurement for HPHT or flux h-BN is in the library. A disordered fraction $f$ changes a fiber result by about $f$ times the $p=1$ column. For the oriented crystal it would also add diffuse rod intensity between the Bragg points at the mounted azimuth.
