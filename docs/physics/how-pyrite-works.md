# How PyRITE works

Follow a case from incident electrons to source spectra and detector predictions.
To run one, use the [Python tutorial](../guides/python-api-workflow.md) or
[CLI quickstart](../guides/getting-started.md).

## From a case to a result

```{figure} ../_static/figures/simulation-pipeline.svg
:name: fig-workflow-simulation-pipeline
:alt: Five stages connect case inputs to electron transport, source scoring, retained results and a downstream observation model.
:width: 720px

Schematic workflow, generated by `scripts/docs_figures.py`; no simulated or
measured data. Stages describe data flow; a command need not execute them all.
```

1. **Describe the case.** `Beam` and the target define entrance phase space,
   material and geometry; `Physics` chooses models, `Numerics` sampling and
   resolution. [Scene recipes](../reference/python-scene-recipes.md) and
   [campaign configuration](../reference/campaign-configuration.md) cover scalar and campaign inputs.
2. **Transport electrons.** Scattering, energy loss and boundaries produce
   flight histories. The [transport contract](../physics/beam-transport/transport-outputs.md)
   defines their positions, directions, energies and timing.
3. **Score source spectra.** Radiation models evaluate PXR/CBS, bremsstrahlung
   and characteristic components on photon-energy grids. The
   [capability map](../physics/capabilities.md) lists optional models,
   experimental status and execution restrictions.
4. **Retain results.** `simulate` returns an in-memory `Result` without
   checkpoint I/O. Profile runs save resumable component checkpoints;
   full histories require separate opt-in [trajectory capture](../reference/trajectory-workflows.md).
   [Working with results](../guides/working-with-results.md) plots actual saved components.
5. **Form an observation.** Acceptance, filters, response and acquisition
   produce detector predictions. [Observation recipes](../reference/python-scene-recipes.md#compose-independent-observations)
   and the [detector model](../physics/detectors/detector-response.md) define
   these operations. Stored checkpoints and trajectories can support reuse.

## Keep scientific boundaries explicit

| Quantity | How to read it |
| --- | --- |
| Source density | Escaped photons/(incident electron · eV · sr), before detector acceptance and response. Integrating over energy removes `/eV`. |
| Detector prediction | Check acceptance, filters, response, current and exposure. Pixel-accepted spectra include solid angle; `PlanarDetector` scalar averages include filter attenuation and remain per sr. |
| Counting observation | Expected registered counts, optionally accompanied by a labelled seeded Poisson realization; neither is experimental data. |

[Spectral conventions](../physics/radiation-physics/spectral-observables.md) and
[scene-result interpretation](../reference/python-scene-recipes.md#interpret-the-result)
define normalization. Align component grids using their
[node/bin semantics](../physics/radiation-physics/energy-grid-semantics.md)
before combining or integrating arrays. PXR-only plus CBS-only diagnostics omit
interference and generally do not equal the combined line spectrum.

A quickstart demonstrates execution. Quantitative use also requires sampling,
grid and reflection convergence, appropriate model assumptions, and evidence
for the intended observable. **Implemented does not mean experimentally
validated.** The [validation overview](../validation/index.md) links that
evidence; the [methodology](../validation/methodology.md) distinguishes
derivation, independent verification, anchors and human sign-off.

## Read the geometry in the right frame

The beam fixes lab $+z$; sample depth follows the surface normal. Tilting the
sample changes their relationship. The [frame diagram](../physics/geometry/transport-geometry.md#frames-and-the-entrance-plane)
and [tilt convention](../physics/geometry/tilt-convention.md) define the axes
and angle signs. Crystal-cut and [reflection selection](../physics/materials/reflection-selection.md)
determine reciprocal vectors; a surface normal alone does not.
