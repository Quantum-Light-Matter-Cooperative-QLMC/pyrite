# Hard BremsLib photon events

The default continuum estimator scores BremsLib emission from electron track length after transport; it does not remove photon energy from the electron. The optional `simulate_trajectories(radiative_model="bremslib-soft-hard", radiative_cutoff_eV=..., bremslib_tables=...)` couples BremsLib electron-atom bremsstrahlung to transport. Photons below a positive cutoff $k_c$ contribute continuous radiative stopping; photons at or above it are sampled as individual hard events. Both sides use the same BremsLib single differential cross section (SDCS) and its direction-resolved double differential cross section (DDCS){cite:p}`poskus2025bremslib`. See [Bremsstrahlung](bremsstrahlung.md#default-bremslib-direction-resolved-backend) for the source tables and angular interpolation.

## Energy partition

At electron energy $T$, the hard-event cross section is $\sigma_h=\int_{k_c}^{T}(d\sigma/dk)\,dk$. The soft radiative stopping cross section is $S_s=\int_0^{k_c}k(d\sigma/dk)\,dk$; the hard first moment is $S_h=\int_{k_c}^{T}k(d\sigma/dk)\,dk$. Thus $S_s+S_h$ is the full BremsLib radiative first moment, independent of the split cutoff. Each element's number density turns these cross sections into a hard-event rate and a continuous energy-loss rate per length; compounds sum element contributions. The hard rate vanishes at $k_c=T$, while the full first moment remains in the soft part. The zeroth moment is formed only above positive $k_c$ because the BremsLib SDCS has a low-energy $1/k$ behavior. Validation: `bremslib-radiative-partition`.

SBETHE `stp.dat` contains **collision** stopping, not radiative stopping. In the continuous inelastic mode the row's mean loss is SBETHE collision loss plus the BremsLib soft radiative loss. With [shell soft/hard inelastic transport](../beam-transport/inelastic-scattering-events.md), the collision contribution is instead its soft shell part, with hard shell transfers sampled separately. No unrestricted radiative stopping term is added on top of this partition.

## What a hard photon changes

The hard-photon hazard competes with elastic, and when enabled hard inelastic, hazards. At an event, the sampled photon energy $k\ge k_c$ ends the electron flight and is subtracted from its post-continuous-loss energy. The next flight starts at the same place and time with energy lower by $k$; the electron direction is unchanged. An event that brings the electron to its cutoff remains recorded on a terminal `CUTOFF` row. Other photon events carry `EVENT_HARD_RADIATIVE`. The row stores the photon energy `hard_radiative_k_eV`, emitting element `hard_radiative_Z`, sampled direction, and target recoil momentum. BremsLib supplies the photon angle relative to the incoming electron direction, with uniform azimuth. Since its DDCS gives no joint electron/photon angular distribution, the model assigns residual momentum to an infinitely heavy target and neglects its recoil energy. Validation: `bremslib-radiative-partition`, `bremslib-radiative-event-spectrum`.

The emitted photon is **scored, not transported**. For a planar slab, `mc_soft_brem_spectrum` scores the track-length contribution below $k_c$, and `mc_hard_brem_event_spectrum` bins sampled photons above it. Their sum gives the coupled continuum on a common grid whose bin edge equals $k_c$. The hard-event point-detector weight is the conditional angular density $(d^2\sigma/dk\,d\Omega)/(d\sigma/dk)$ times the escape transmission $e^{-\mu(k)L}$. Event frequency has already been sampled, so the scorer adds no second number-density, path-length, or cross-section factor. It returns photons per incident electron per eV per steradian. The scorer includes hard photons on terminal cutoff rows and currently supports one planar slab, without layered or finite-footprint escape. Calling the uncoupled `mc_brem_spectrum` on coupled rows is rejected to prevent double counting. Validation: `bremslib-radiative-event-spectrum`.

## Photon interaction boundary

Emitted photons are scored, not transported. A hard photon's only effect on the electron is the energy debit above, so its later fate cannot change the electron track. Each photon reaches the detector through the narrow-beam escape factor {math}`\exp(-\mu(k)L_{\rm escape})` of [photon escape](photon-escape-and-dispersion.md), where {math}`\mu` includes photoabsorption and Rayleigh and Compton scattering. A photon that interacts is removed; nothing is returned to the ray. The mode therefore omits:

- scattered photons that still reach the detector (no build-up factor);
- fluorescence and photoelectrons from photoabsorption, including characteristic lines excited by bremsstrahlung rather than by the beam;
- pair production above {math}`2m_ec^2 = 1.022` MeV, both as a secondary process and as a term in {math}`\mu`;
- photon energy deposited in the target.

These omissions scale with the probability that a photon interacts before escaping. For normal escape through the full thickness, `materials.attenuation` gives at most {math}`4.3\times10^{-6}` for silicon and HOPG and {math}`2.5\times10^{-4}` for WSe₂ through 1000 Å between 100 and 790 keV. Through 100 µm it is at most {math}`4.3\times10^{-3}` for silicon and HOPG, but 0.22 for WSe₂ at 100 keV. The scored-only treatment is therefore accurate for thin targets and becomes an explicit error term for thick, high-{math}`Z` ones.

The attenuation tables also end: the Elam scattering data at `ELAM_E_MAX_EV` = 800 keV and Chantler photoabsorption near 966 keV. Above 800 keV {math}`\mu` is NaN. `mc_hard_brem_event_spectrum`, like the wide soft grid, treats an unavailable {math}`\mu` as zero, so photons above 800 keV currently escape with unit transmission. No catalogue grid reaches that energy (the widest `E_grid_brem` stops at 262.4 keV). A coupled run with MeV photons nevertheless has an undefined escape factor. Multi-MeV use of this mode needs an extended attenuation table that includes pair production, not only the coupled electron treatment.

## Straggling and supported scope

This coupled radiative mode requires `energy_model="midpoint"`, an ungrooved target, and `straggling=False`; the exact CPU and CUDA cores support it, while the transport LUT does not. The soft radiative first moment is a deterministic continuous debit, and the hard photon energy is stochastic. There is no separate radiative straggling sampler for photons below $k_c$. If shell soft/hard inelastic transport is also enabled, its optional PENELOPE soft-loss straggling is therefore disabled by the same `straggling=False` requirement; hard inelastic events remain sampled. See [Inelastic scattering and energy-loss straggling](../beam-transport/inelastic-scattering-events.md).

BremsLib omits electron-electron bremsstrahlung. The [validation ledger](../../validation/physics-validation-ledger.md) records the numerical comparisons and remaining scope limits. Neither this event model nor its Geant4 comparison establishes general multi-MeV full-track validity.
