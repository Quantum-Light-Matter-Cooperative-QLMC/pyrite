# Hard BremsLib photon events

Production runs couple BremsLib emission to electron transport when tables resolve; direct transport calls couple when tables are supplied. The explicit `simulate_trajectories(radiative_model="bremslib-soft-hard", radiative_cutoff_eV=..., bremslib_tables=...)` couples BremsLib electron-atom bremsstrahlung to transport. Photons below a positive cutoff $k_c$ contribute continuous radiative stopping; photons at or above it are sampled as individual hard events. Both sides use the same BremsLib single differential cross section (SDCS) and its direction-resolved double differential cross section (DDCS){cite:p}`poskus2025bremslib`. See [Bremsstrahlung](bremsstrahlung.md#default-bremslib-direction-resolved-backend) for the source tables and angular interpolation.

Production runs may override the automatic mode with `Numerics(radiative_model="bremslib-soft-hard", radiative_cutoff_eV=...)` or `pyrite profile numerics set --radiative-model bremslib-soft-hard --radiative-cutoff-ev EV`. It requires midpoint energy integration and BremsLib tables for every layer element. A grooved case warns and uses uncoupled scoring with no coupling in its identity until grooved hard-photon scoring is implemented (#199). Under `bremsstrahlung_model="auto"`, a case whose tables do not all resolve falls back to uncoupled EEDL scoring, and its identity records no coupling. $k_c$ may not exceed the continuum electron cutoff (1 keV by default), so it usually lies below the continuum grid and every scored bin is hard. The runner, brem repair and energy-grid convergence paths score coupled cases with the expected-value estimator below. The default couples BremsLib when tables resolve; grooved targets and EEDL fallback use uncoupled scoring.

## Energy partition

At electron energy $T$, the hard-event cross section is $\sigma_h=\int_{k_c}^{T}(d\sigma/dk)\,dk$. The soft radiative stopping cross section is $S_s=\int_0^{k_c}k(d\sigma/dk)\,dk$; the hard first moment is $S_h=\int_{k_c}^{T}k(d\sigma/dk)\,dk$. Thus $S_s+S_h$ is the full BremsLib radiative first moment, independent of the split cutoff. Each element's number density turns these cross sections into a hard-event rate and a continuous energy-loss rate per length; compounds sum element contributions. The hard rate vanishes at $k_c=T$, while the full first moment remains in the soft part. The zeroth moment is formed only above positive $k_c$ because the BremsLib SDCS has a low-energy $1/k$ behavior. Validation: `bremslib-radiative-partition`.

SBETHE `stp.dat` contains **collision** stopping, not radiative stopping. In the continuous inelastic mode the row's mean loss is SBETHE collision loss plus the BremsLib soft radiative loss. With [shell soft/hard inelastic transport](../beam-transport/inelastic-scattering-events.md), the collision contribution is instead its soft shell part, with hard shell transfers sampled separately. No unrestricted radiative stopping term is added on top of this partition.

## What a hard photon changes

The hard-photon hazard competes with elastic, and when enabled hard inelastic, hazards. At an event, the sampled photon energy $k\ge k_c$ ends the electron flight and is subtracted from its post-continuous-loss energy. The next flight starts at the same place and time with energy lower by $k$; the electron direction is unchanged. An event that brings the electron to its cutoff remains recorded on a terminal `CUTOFF` row. Other photon events carry `EVENT_HARD_RADIATIVE`. The row stores the photon energy `hard_radiative_k_eV`, emitting element `hard_radiative_Z`, sampled direction, and target recoil momentum. BremsLib supplies the photon angle relative to the incoming electron direction, with uniform azimuth. Since its DDCS gives no joint electron/photon angular distribution, the model assigns residual momentum to an infinitely heavy target and neglects its recoil energy. Validation: `bremslib-radiative-partition`, `bremslib-radiative-event-spectrum`.

The emitted photon is **scored, not transported**. `mc_soft_brem_spectrum` scores the track-length contribution below $k_c$, and `mc_hard_brem_event_spectrum` bins sampled photons above it. Their sum gives the coupled continuum on a common grid. When $k_c$ lies inside a bin, the soft node estimate is weighted by that bin's below-cutoff width and hard events fill the remainder; a cutoff below the grid makes every bin hard, and one above it every bin soft. The hard-event point-detector weight is the conditional angular density $(d^2\sigma/dk\,d\Omega)/(d\sigma/dk)$ times the escape transmission $\exp(-\sum_l \mu_l(k)L_l)$, where $L_l$ is the path from the event endpoint through layer $l$ toward the detector. The path uses the same planar-layer and finite-rectangular-footprint geometry as the track-length scorers; grooved faces are not supported. Event frequency has already been sampled, so the scorer adds no second number-density, path-length, or cross-section factor. It returns photons per incident electron per eV per steradian. The scorer includes hard photons on terminal cutoff rows. Calling the uncoupled `mc_brem_spectrum` on coupled rows is rejected to prevent double counting. Validation: `bremslib-radiative-event-spectrum`.

## Production continuum estimator

Production does not histogram the sampled photons. At catalog electron counts (150 continuum electrons per case) a 30–300 keV beam emits a handful of hard photons, so the event estimate above $k_c$ is almost empty. `mc_coupled_brem_spectrum` instead scores the full BremsLib DDCS by track length along the coupled electron tracks, exactly as the uncoupled `mc_brem_spectrum` scores its own tracks, over soft and hard photon energies alike.

This is the expectation of the analog soft-plus-event sum. Along a track parameterized by path length $s$, hard photons are a point process in $(s,k)$ with intensity $\lambda(s,k)=\sum_Z n_Z\,d\sigma_Z/dk\,(T(s^-))$ for $k\ge k_c$. The intensity depends only on the track before $s$, as does the event weight $g$ (angular density toward the detector and escape transmission from the emission point). The compensator identity for such a process gives $E[\sum_i g(s_i,k_i)] = E[\int ds\int_{k_c} dk\,\lambda(s,k)\,g(s,k)]$, where both expectations run over the same coupled track law. With $g$ the conditional angular density $(d^2\sigma/dk\,d\Omega)/(d\sigma/dk)$ times the escape transmission, $\lambda g$ is the DDCS times the transmission, so the right side is the track-length estimate on the same tracks. Below $k_c$ no photon is sampled and the track-length term is the definition of the soft part. The sampled events still shape the tracks through their energy debit; they are only not counted again as photons, so nothing is double counted. The variance is not lower by Rao–Blackwell: a coupled track records every photon's energy debit, so conditioning on it returns the analog sum. Writing the analog sum as $H=A+M$, with $A$ the expected-value estimate and $M$ the compensated martingale, $\operatorname{Var}H=\operatorname{Var}A+E\int\lambda g^2+2\operatorname{Cov}(A,M)$; $\operatorname{Var}A\le\tfrac14E\int\lambda g^2$ suffices for $\operatorname{Var}H\ge\operatorname{Var}A$. Per electron and per bin this reads $\operatorname{Var}\nu_e\le\tfrac14E[\nu_e]$, with $\nu_e$ the electron's expected hard-photon count in the bin; on a 100 keV silicon run $\operatorname{Var}\nu_e/E[\nu_e]$ is $2\times10^{-10}$–$4\times10^{-8}$ per keV bin.

The two scorers differ only in quadrature. Transport draws each row's hard-event distance at the row-start energy and the photon energy at the pre-event energy, so its realized intensity is $\mu_h(T_{\rm start})\,p(k\mid T(s^-))$ rather than $\lambda(s,k)$. The track-length scorer evaluates the cross section at each segment's midpoint energy and averages escape along the segment (`segment-escape-average`), while the event scorer uses the emission point. The difference is first order in the energy lost per row and vanishes as the steps shrink; on production numerics it is $-2.7\times10^{-4}$ (Si, 100 keV) and $-9\times10^{-4}$ (C, 30 keV), independent of photon energy. The midpoint track-length side is the more accurate of the two. `mc_soft_brem_spectrum` and `mc_hard_brem_event_spectrum` remain as the analog estimator for validation. Validation: `bremslib-coupled-expected-spectrum`.

## Photon interaction boundary

Emitted photons are scored, not transported. A hard photon's only effect on the electron is the energy debit above, so its later fate cannot change the electron track. Each photon reaches the detector through the narrow-beam escape factor {math}`\exp(-\mu(k)L_{\rm escape})` of [photon escape](photon-escape-and-dispersion.md), where {math}`\mu` includes photoabsorption and Rayleigh and Compton scattering. A photon that interacts is removed; nothing is returned to the ray. The mode therefore omits:

- scattered photons that still reach the detector (no build-up factor);
- fluorescence and photoelectrons from photoabsorption, including characteristic lines excited by bremsstrahlung rather than by the beam;
- pair production above {math}`2m_ec^2 = 1.022` MeV as a secondary process (it is a term in {math}`\mu`, which removes the photon from the ray), unless the opt-in [pair conversion](#pair-conversion-of-hard-photons) below is enabled;
- photon energy deposited in the target.

These omissions scale with the probability that a photon interacts before escaping. For normal escape through the full thickness, `materials.attenuation` gives at most {math}`4.3\times10^{-6}` for silicon and HOPG and {math}`2.5\times10^{-4}` for WSe₂ through 1000 Å between 100 keV and 10 MeV. Through 100 µm it is at most {math}`4.3\times10^{-3}` for silicon and HOPG, but 0.22 for WSe₂ at 100 keV. The scored-only treatment is therefore accurate for thin targets and becomes an explicit error term for thick, high-{math}`Z` ones.

The escape coefficient {math}`\mu` is the EPDL2025 narrow-beam total (photoelectric, coherent, incoherent, and pair production in the nuclear and electron fields), defined from 1 eV to 100 GeV (issue #274; Validation: `narrow-beam-total-attenuation`). MeV photons therefore get a defined escape factor that includes the pair term. Outside that band {math}`\mu` is NaN, and `mc_hard_brem_event_spectrum`, like the wide soft grid, raises rather than reading it as transparency. Before #274, {math}`\mu` ended at 800 keV and photons above it escaped with unit transmission.

## Pair conversion of hard photons

`simulate_trajectories(pair_production_model="penelope-2024", secondary_threshold_eV=...)` lets coupled hard photons above {math}`2m_ec^2` create electron–positron pairs inside the target (issue #275). It requires the [secondary cascade](../beam-transport/shell-soft-hard-transport.md#secondary-electron-transport), so it inherits the cascade's material coverage (catalog crystals with shell data), and coupled BremsLib transport without grooves. `None` (the default) leaves every photon radiated, as above.

**First interaction.** A photon of energy {math}`k>2m_ec^2` leaves its row endpoint {math}`\mathbf r_{\rm mid}+\tfrac12L\hat{\mathbf v}` at clock {math}`t_{\rm end}` along its sampled direction {math}`\hat{\mathbf n}`. Along that ray the layers have EPDL2025 narrow-beam coefficients {math}`\mu_l(k)=\sum_i n_{i,l}\,\sigma_{{\rm tot},i}(k)`, crossed over path intervals {math}`[s_l^-,s_l^+]` capped at the first planar or side face. One draw {math}`\tau=-\ln(1-\xi)` locates the first interaction where the cumulative optical depth {math}`\sum_l\mu_l\,|[s_l^-,s_l^+]\cap[0,s]|` reaches {math}`\tau`. If the ray leaves first, the photon escapes. At an interaction in layer {math}`l` the channel is pair with probability {math}`\mu_{{\rm pair},l}/\mu_l`, where {math}`\mu_{{\rm pair},l}=\sum_i n_{i,l}(\sigma_{{\rm pair,nuc},i}+\sigma_{{\rm pair,el},i})`. The atom is element {math}`i` with probability {math}`n_{i,l}\sigma_{{\rm pair},i}/\mu_{{\rm pair},l}`. The photon travels at {math}`c`, so the pair starts at clock {math}`t_{\rm end}+s` (Å, {math}`c=1`). The first interaction uses {math}`\mu_{\rm tot}` rather than {math}`\mu_{\rm pair}` alone, so pairs compete correctly with photoabsorption and Compton and Rayleigh scattering. A non-pair first interaction is not followed and the photon stays radiated: there is no general photon transport. Validation: `photon-pair-first-interaction`.

**Pair kinematics.** The electron takes {math}`E_-=\epsilon k-m_ec^2` and the positron {math}`E_+=k-E_--2m_ec^2`, so {math}`k=E_-+E_++2m_ec^2` exactly. The reduced energy {math}`\epsilon` on {math}`(\kappa^{-1},1-\kappa^{-1})`, {math}`\kappa=k/m_ec^2`, follows PENELOPE-2024's Bethe–Heitler DCS with exponential screening, Coulomb correction and an empirical low-energy term {cite:p}`salvat2024penelope`:

```{math}
p(\epsilon)\propto 2\left(\tfrac12-\epsilon\right)^2\phi_1(\epsilon)+\phi_2(\epsilon),\qquad
\phi_{1,2}=\max\!\left[g_{1,2}(b)+g_0(\kappa),\,0\right],\qquad
b=\frac{Rm_ec}{\hbar}\,\frac{1}{2\kappa\,\epsilon(1-\epsilon)},
```

with {math}`g_0=4\ln(Rm_ec/\hbar)-4f_C(Z)+F_0(\kappa,Z)` (Eqs. 2.85–2.88; screening radii from Table 2.2). It is sampled exactly by PENELOPE's composition and rejection method (Eqs. 2.91–2.96). Integrating the DCS with the triplet factor {math}`\eta=0` reproduces EPDL2025's nuclear-field pair cross section within 12 % at 1.5 MeV, 5 % at 2 MeV and 4 % from 3 to 10 MeV for C, Si, Mo and Pb. This matches the manual's own statement against Hubbell et al. Only the DCS shape is used. Each particle's polar angle about {math}`\hat{\mathbf n}` follows the leading high-energy term {math}`p(\cos\theta_\pm)\propto(1-\beta_\pm\cos\theta_\pm)^{-2}` (Eq. 2.99), with independent uniform azimuths. Validation: `pair-production-sampling`.

**Triplet production** enters only through the EPDL total: as in PENELOPE, it is simulated as a pair with the nuclear-field kinematics, and the recoil electron is not created. For catalog elements its share {math}`\sigma_{\rm pair,el}/(\sigma_{\rm pair,nuc}+\sigma_{\rm pair,el})` is zero below {math}`4m_ec^2`. At 3 / 5 MeV it is 2.6 / 9.0 % for Li, 1.3 / 4.7 % for C, 0.6 / 2.1 % for Si and at most 0.2 / 0.7 % from Mo upward. The model therefore omits a recoil electron in at most a few percent of low-{math}`Z` conversions. Energy stays conserved because the recoil's share is given to the pair. The Bethe–Heitler DCS is symmetric in {math}`\epsilon\leftrightarrow1-\epsilon`, so the low-energy electron/positron asymmetry from the nuclear field is neglected (PENELOPE §2.4).

**Daughters.** A pair electron with {math}`E_->T_s` is launched in the next cascade generation from the interaction point with the sampled direction, and is marked `launch_kind = 2` in `secondary_tracks` (shell secondaries are 1, primaries 0); its `parent_hard_ordinal` holds the photon ordinal on the parent track. Its stream key hashes (seed, parent track, photon ordinal) under its own salt. A smaller {math}`E_-` is deposited locally. The positron is **recorded, not transported** until positron transport lands (#276). The run warns, and `result["pair_production"]` carries `positrons_transported = False`, every event (row, photon energy, atom, point, clock, both energies and directions, launched track), and photon outcome counts. `secondary_energy_balance` removes each converted photon from `radiated` and adds `pair_rest_mass` ({math}`2m_ec^2`), `positron` ({math}`E_+`) and `pair_subthreshold` (part of `deposited`), so the balance still closes per history.

**RNG.** Each photon owns a Philox stream keyed on (seed, parent track, photon ordinal on that track). Its outcome is therefore independent of batching, generation order and transport core. The step runs on the host after photon completion, which is already host-side in this mode. Identical transport rows therefore give identical pairs on CPU and CUDA cores.

**Magnitude.** Conversion needs both a hard photon above 1.022 MeV and a pair interaction before it leaves the target. At REGAE-scale beam energies (≤5 MeV) both factors are small for thin targets. Pairs matter only for thick targets and MeV beams. The production continuum scorer is a track-length estimator of the BremsLib DDCS, with escape transmission that already removes converted photons. Pair conversion therefore changes scored spectra only through the radiation of the launched daughter electrons.

## Straggling and supported scope

This coupled radiative mode requires `energy_model="midpoint"` and an ungrooved target; the exact CPU and CUDA cores support it, while the transport LUT does not. With `straggling=True`, a row samples collision loss using the existing Urban law, or PENELOPE's soft collision law when shell soft/hard transport is enabled, then adds the BremsLib soft first moment as a deterministic loss. For row length $s$, the sampled continuous debit is $\Delta E_{\rm coll}+sS_{\rm rad,soft}$, with mean $s(S_{\rm coll}+S_{\rm rad,soft})$. Hard photons debit energy as separate stochastic events. The unresolved soft radiative variance is neglected; its second moment is bounded by $k_c S_{\rm rad,soft}s$ because every soft photon has $k<k_c$. At zero BremsLib cross section the collision-only straggled tracks are recovered. Validation: `bremslib-radiative-partition`. See [Inelastic scattering and energy-loss straggling](../beam-transport/inelastic-scattering-events.md).

BremsLib omits electron-electron bremsstrahlung. The [validation ledger](../../validation/physics-validation-ledger.md) records the numerical comparisons and remaining scope limits. Neither this event model nor its Geant4 comparison establishes general multi-MeV full-track validity.
