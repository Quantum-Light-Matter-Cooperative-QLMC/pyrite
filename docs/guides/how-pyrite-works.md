# How PyRITE works

PyRITE connects an incident electron beam and a material target to predicted
radiation spectra, then applies detector and acquisition models where requested.
This guide names the stages so that a source spectrum, a stored checkpoint and a
measured-energy histogram can be interpreted in context. For commands to run your
first case, start with [Getting started](getting-started.md).

## From a case to a result

```{figure} ../_static/figures/simulation-pipeline.svg
Optional models and execution restrictions are listed in the
[capability map](../physics/capabilities.md).
```

1. **Describe the case.** A `Beam` describes entrance energy and phase space.
   The target supplies material, thickness, orientation and footprint. Observation
   geometry specifies where radiation is evaluated. `Physics` selects models;
   `Numerics` selects sampling and resolution controls. A named profile resolves
   these choices across a sweep; the scalar Python API constructs one case.
2. **Transport the electrons.** Sampled entrance states become material flight
   segments with energies, directions, positions and timing. Scattering, energy
   loss and boundaries determine the histories. The
   [transport output contract](../physics/beam-transport/transport-outputs.md)
   defines the returned fields; [transport geometry](../physics/geometry/transport-geometry.md)
   defines entry, exit and missed-incidence accounting.
3. **Score radiation.** The radiation models consume transport histories and
   evaluate spectra on photon-energy grids. PXR/CBS line radiation,
   bremsstrahlung and characteristic radiation remain distinct components.
   Selected optional models add their own outputs and restrictions. Read the
   [capability map](../physics/capabilities.md) before assuming that an optional
   source is supported by every execution or observation path.
4. **Retain the result.** `simulate` returns an in-memory `Result` without
   checkpoint I/O. Profile runs retain resumable component checkpoints under
   `pyrite-output/checkpoints/`. Checkpoints record spectra and provenance;
   complete electron histories require opt-in trajectory capture. See
   [Working with results](working-with-results.md) for component layout,
   identity, inspection and reuse.
5. **Form an observation.** Acceptance, filter attenuation, detector response
   and acquisition turn the appropriate source outputs into detector predictions.
   Depending on the path, this may be an analysis view or a separately stored
   counting observation. The [detector response model](../physics/detectors/detector-response.md)
   owns those transformations; [pixel inspection](working-with-results.md#pixel-detector-observations)
   distinguishes expected counts from a seeded Poisson realization.

The arrows in {numref}`fig-workflow-simulation-pipeline` describe data ownership,
not a promise that every command executes every stage. Existing checkpoints and
captured trajectories allow some later stages to reuse earlier work.

## Read the geometry in the right frame

The lab origin is where the nominal beam axis intersects the entrance surface.
The beam defines lab $+z$; lab $+x$ points transversely toward the detector.
The detector direction and beam axis define the lab scattering plane. The
observation angle is measured from lab $+z$ toward the detector direction.
These conventions are defined in [Sample tilt](../physics/geometry/tilt-convention.md).

The sample frame follows the target: its entrance is at $z=0$, and depth
increases along sample $+z$. At normal incidence the nominal beam and sample
normal align. A tilted target changes their relationship without rotating the
lab beam axis. At zero tilt azimuth, positive polar tilt moves the sample normal
toward the detector; azimuth rotates that tilt about the beam axis. The diagram
shows only the zero-azimuth section, with a separate normal-incidence sample view
for reading depth.

Do not infer a diffracting reciprocal-vector direction from the drawn surface
normal for arbitrary crystal cuts. Use the selected
[crystal structure and orientation](../physics/materials/crystal-structure.md)
and [reflection selection](../physics/materials/reflection-selection.md).

## Keep scientific boundaries explicit

A **source yield** is not a detector count rate. Source spectra are normalized
per incident electron and carry photon-energy and solid-angle conventions.
Beam current, acquisition time and detector response belong downstream. Grid
entries may be evaluation nodes or bin edges; follow
[energy-grid semantics](../physics/radiation-physics/energy-grid-semantics.md)
before integrating or combining arrays. PXR-only and CBS-only diagnostic curves
also omit their mutual interference term; their sum is not the combined line
spectrum. These contracts are collected in
[Spectral observables](../physics/radiation-physics/spectral-observables.md).

A **numerical setting** controls sampling or resolution; it does not establish
physical validity. A quickstart result demonstrates the workflow. For a
scientific prediction, check the relevant model assumptions, sampling and grid
convergence, and the evidence for the observable you intend to use.

An **implemented model** is not automatically a reproduced experiment. The
[validation overview](../validation/index.md) leads to generated status summaries,
domain inventories and detailed evidence. The
[validation methodology](../validation/methodology.md) distinguishes derivation,
independent verification, anchors and formal human sign-off. Follow the relevant
record rather than treating a single status as validation of the whole program.
