## `cr-debye-b-of-t`

- **Claim:** site Debye–Waller `B(T) = 8π²⟨u_x²⟩` is the linear interpolation of phonon-DOS tabulated rows inside their range. Outside the range it is the Debye model `B = (6h²/(m_u A k_B))(T/Θ²)[φ(Θ/T) + Θ/(4T)]`, with `φ(y) = (1/y)∫₀ʸ x/(eˣ−1) dx` and `Θ` fitted to the nearest tabulated end.
- **Code:** `materials/channeling/thermal.py::{debye_integral,debye_b_factor,fit_debye_temperature,ThermalSite.b_factor,thermal_b_factor}`; `data/channeling_thermal.toml`
- **Source:** Willis & Pryor (1975); Peng, Ren, Dudarev & Whelan, Acta Cryst. A52, 456 (1996), Eq. 8 and Table 1 ([doi:10.1107/S010876739600089X](https://doi.org/10.1107/S010876739600089X))
- **Status:** anchored
- **Checks:** Units: Å² from the CODATA prefactor 6h²/(m_u k_B) = 11 490 Å²·K. Peng quotes 11 492; `φ(y→0) = 1`; `φ(y) → π²/(6y)`; `φ(10)` equals 0.16444 by independent quadrature; Classical `B ∝ T`; T-independent zero-point limit; The fitted Θ_D reproduces its anchor exactly. Si Θ_D = 520 K, in line with Peng's high-temperature ≈500 K; Tabulated rows are reproduced exactly, and the model is continuous at both range ends.
- **Anchor:** `tests/materials/test_channeling_profile.py::{test_debye_integral_limits,test_debye_b_factor_classical_and_zero_point_limits,test_fit_debye_temperature_round_trips,test_thermal_rows_reproduced_and_extrapolation_continuous,test_diamond_b_factor_matches_xray_measurements}`
- **Notes:** **Data.** Rows 170–280 K are transcribed from Peng Table 1. Rows below 170 K were ambiguous in the published layout and were not used; **Why interpolation.** A one-parameter Debye fit at 280 K is 11% low for Si at 180 K but only −1.6% for diamond, matching Peng's warning that the Debye model fails for Si. The model is therefore used only outside the tabulated range. Cryogenic Si values carry that model error; **Catalog discrepancy.** The PDOS diamond value (0.144 Å² at 293 K) disagrees with the catalog `B_ang2 = 0.21` used by PXR/CBS. That is a `debye-waller-catalog-provenance` follow-up, not changed here. Two X-ray measurements support the PDOS value: Takama et al., Acta Cryst. A 46, 514 (1990), abstract, B = 0.142(9) Å² (Pendellösung), and Bindzus et al., Acta Cryst. A 70, 39 (2014), SI Table 2, U_iso = 0.001808(10) Å² (B = 0.1427 Å²); **2026-10-15 fresh-context verdict `rederived`** ([write-up](channeling/cr-debye-b-of-t.md)): CODATA prefactor 11 490.29 Å²·K vs Peng's rounded 11 492 (0.015%); B(T) identical to an independent quad/brentq reference to ~16 digits over 10–1000 K; all 20 TOML rows (170–280 K) match Peng Table 1; rows below 170 K unconfirmable in the extract (slope break at 160 K supports excluding them). `φ(10) = 0.164443` by independent quadrature; Peng's Table 3 is not legible in the extract, so the earlier "matches Peng's tabulated value" wording is withdrawn. Human sign-off pending.

## `cr-mott-bethe-factor`

- **Claim:** the electron–atom potential Fourier component uses the Mott–Bethe numerator `Z − f0(g)` over `g²` (Poisson's equation), with only the energy-independent X-ray form factor `f0` (Waasmaier–Kirfel, valid to `s ≤ 6 Å⁻¹`). The anomalous `f′, f″` are excluded.
- **Code:** `materials/channeling/potential.py::planar_profile`
- **Source:** Mott–Bethe formula, `f^e(s) = (Z − f_x(s))/(8π² a₀ s²)` (International Tables Vol. C, §4.3); Andersen et al., Phys. Scr. 28, 308 (1983)
- **Status:** anchored
- **Checks:** Harmonics are kept up to `s = g/(4π) ≤ 6 Å⁻¹`, and `g = 0` is excluded, so the `s → 0` limit is never evaluated; The diamond (220) coefficient equals `−U_g` exactly, since `U_g` uses bare `f0` for non-edge-prone elements.
- **Anchor:** `tests/materials/test_channeling_profile.py::{test_planar_coefficient_is_negative_cbs_coupling,test_si111_coefficient_against_cbed_structure_factors,test_diamond_coefficients_against_xray_structure_factors}`
- **Notes:** **Finding.** `materials.crystal.U_g` adds `f′` for `_EDGE_PRONE` elements (Si, P, Fe, …) even with `use_henke=False`. For Si (220) that moves the CBS potential coupling 3.7% from the electrostatic `Z − f0` value. That belongs to the CBS model (`cbs-amplitude`) and is reported for review there; **CBED anchor.** Zuo, Microsc. Res. Tech. 46, 220 (1999), Table 1 (Si, room temperature, 196.7 kV): U(111) = 0.04736(7) Å⁻², U(222) = 0.921(50)×10⁻³ Å⁻². With `U_g = 2mγeV_g/h²` (Zuo & Spence, Ultramicroscopy 35, 185 (1991)) these are V(111) = 5.144 V and V(222) = 0.100 V. The independent-atom |U_111| = 5.444 eV is 5.8% above the measurement, which Zuo's Table 2 X-ray Pendellösung value (0.4736(4)) confirms; the gap is bonding charge, to which the low-order `Z − f0` difference is most sensitive. (222) is forbidden for spherical harmonic atoms, so the model gives 0 against the measured 0.100 V (2% of V(111)); **Diamond X-ray anchor.** Bindzus et al., Acta Cryst. A 70, 39 (2014), SI §1.4 (HC/Rietveld extracted F, thermally smeared with U_iso = 0.001808 Å²), converted by Mott–Bethe on the measured charge, `|U_g| = 4πe²(Z|S|T − F)/(V g²)`. The model (220), (440) and (660) are +0.2%, −0.3% and −0.5% from it, while (111) is 10.6% high, the same bonding signature as Si(111). Takama et al. (1990) has nine Pendellösung F(hkl) but no attachment was available, so it is not transcribed; No Doyle–Turner cross-check yet; the Si(110) line-energy anchor (`cr-transverse-eigenproblem`) agrees with Azadegan's Doyle–Turner potential to 0.6%; **2026-10-15 fresh-context verdict `rederived`** ([write-up](channeling/cr-mott-bethe-factor.md)): Poisson + Born gives `u = −4πe²(Z−f0)/q² = −(2πħ²/m) f^e`; `cromer_mann_f0` equals `xraydb.f0` (no `f′/f″`) at s = 0.26–5.9 Å⁻¹; independent Si (220) −4.31575 eV vs code −4.31589 eV (3×10⁻⁵, lattice-constant rounding). Human sign-off pending.

## `cr-planar-continuum-potential`

- **Claim:** the plane-averaged electron potential energy is `U(x) = Σ_{n≠0} U_n e^{i n g x}`, with `U_n = −(4π e²/(V g_n²)) Σ_j (Z_j − f0_j(g_n)) e^{i2π n hkl·R_j} e^{−B_j s_n²}`. The mean inner potential `U_0` is set to 0. The true period comes from the gcd of non-extinct harmonics. The nuclear and electron density coefficients use the same thermal smearing.
- **Code:** `materials/channeling/potential.py::{planar_profile,PlanarProfile.potential}`
- **Source:** Andersen, Bonderup, Lægsgaard & Sørensen, Phys. Scr. 28, 308 (1983); Azadegan, Comput. Phys. Commun. 184, 1064 (2013), Eqs. 4–5
- **Status:** anchored
- **Checks:** Units: eV from e²/(V g²); Electron wells sit on the atomic planes, consistent with the electron charge being −e; Si (110)/(100)/(111) periods equal d₂₂₀, d₄₀₀ and d₁₁₁. Doubled indices give the same profile; The centrosymmetric (110) coefficients are real; Mean densities are 8/V atoms and 112/V electrons; Heating monotonically reduces the well depth.
- **Anchor:** `tests/materials/test_channeling_profile.py::{test_extinctions_set_true_planar_period,test_si110_wells_sit_on_planes_with_literature_depth,test_si111_coefficient_against_cbed_structure_factors,test_diamond_coefficients_against_xray_structure_factors,test_density_means_and_heating}`
- **Notes:** **CBED coefficients.** The Si(111) fundamental is 5.8% above the Zuo (1999) CBED value, and the bonding-only (222) harmonic is absent; see `cr-mott-bethe-factor`. Independent-atom wells are therefore slightly too deep at low order. Diamond (110) harmonics agree with X-ray data to 0.5% and (111) is 10.6% high (Bindzus 2014); see `cr-mott-bethe-factor`; Si(110) depth is 21.0 eV at 293.15 K. Literature reports ~22 eV, and Azadegan 2013 Fig. 1 shows ≈21 eV; Diamond(110) depth is 23.6 eV, and its line energies match Gouanère (1988) to 0.2% at 80 MeV (`cr-transverse-eigenproblem`). The walkthrough's 25–30 eV estimate predates the PDOS `B`; **2026-10-15 fresh-context verdict `rederived`** ([write-up](channeling/cr-planar-continuum-potential.md)): independent 8-atom Si(110) rebuild with `|n| ≤ 46` matches `U_220/U_440/U_660` to ~1e-4 eV, depth 21.039 vs 21.040 eV, `U(x)` to 4e-4 eV; truncating at `|n| ≤ 5` gives only 17.7 eV, so high harmonics carry ~3 eV of the depth. Caveat recorded by the verifier: the period gcd keys on `|U_n|`, not the geometric structure factor, which is safe while `Z − f0 > 0` but would misread an accidental zero. Human sign-off pending.

## `cr-transverse-eigenproblem`

- **Claim:** transverse states solve `[−(ħ²/2γm) d²/dx² + U(x)] ψ = E_⊥ ψ`, expanded in Bloch plane waves, with `H_{mm′} = (ħ²/2γm)(k + m g)² δ_{mm′} + U_{m−m′}`. A state is bound when it lies below `max U`.
- **Code:** `materials/channeling/transverse.py::{transverse_states,TransverseStates.wavefunction}`
- **Source:** Andersen et al., Phys. Scr. 28, 308 (1983); Azadegan (2013), Eqs. 2–6
- **Status:** anchored
- **Checks:** Free limit: the plane-wave spectrum, with no bound states; Cosine (Mathieu) limit: spacings `ħω − (a g²/4)(n+1)` to 2×10⁻⁴; Parity alternates at `k = 0`; Basis convergence between M = 16 and 48; Bound count grows as √γ: 6 → 12 → 24 at 40 → 160 → 640 MeV.
- **Anchor:** `tests/materials/test_channeling_profile.py::{test_free_limit_has_no_bound_states,test_harmonic_limit_gives_equal_spacing,test_parity_alternates_and_selection_rule_emerges,test_bound_state_count_scales_as_sqrt_gamma,test_si110_40mev_matches_azadegan,test_diamond_lines_match_gouanere,test_diamond110_9mev_line_within_bloch_band,test_si_planar_lines_below_watson_detector_cutoff}`
- **Notes:** **Azadegan anchor (Si(110), 40 MeV).** Six bound states, matching the published six. `2γ²(ε₁−ε₀)` = 76.69 keV against the published 76.2071 keV at `k = 1.3089 Å⁻¹`, 0.6% apart. The remaining difference is consistent with Doyle–Turner factors and a different thermal amplitude; **Diamond anchors.** Gouanère et al., Phys. Rev. B 38, 4352 (1988), Table II column 10 (forward `(E^if)_0`, corrected for multiple scattering, room temperature): (110) at 80.23 MeV, 1→0/2→1/3→2/4→3 = 313.4/213.9/164.6/130.1 keV, model within 0.2%; (110) at 53.2 MeV, 1→0/2→1/3→2 = 160.0/103.6/78.0 keV, model 1.1–1.4% low; (100) 1→0 at 53.2/80.23/110.2 MeV = 119.8/239.0/405.0 keV, model 0.3–1.4% low. Genz et al., Phys. Rev. B 53, 8922 (1996), Table VII: (110) 1→0 at 9.0 MeV, 7.80–7.93 keV over four crystals, inside the model's Bloch band 7.51–8.09 keV (k-mean 7.78). At 5.2 MeV, state 1 is at the barrier and the k-mean is 10% low, as is Genz's own calculation; (111) at 9.0 MeV, 1→0 3.14 vs 3.08–3.16 keV, while 3→2 is 7% high, which Genz attributes to bonding in the double well. **Unresolved diamond outlier.** Azadegan, Wagner & Pawelke, Phys. ## `cr-mean-inner-potential`

- **Claim:** the electron's mean potential energy in the crystal is the `g → 0` limit of the planar sum, `U_0 = −(4π e²/V) Σ_j Z_j⟨r²⟩_j/6`, using `f0(g) = Z − Z⟨r²⟩g²/6 + O(g⁴)`. It is the neutral independent-atom value and is negative, so electrons gain `−U_0` on entry.
- **Code:** `materials/channeling/entry.py::mean_inner_potential_eV`
- **Source:** Ibers, Acta Cryst. 11, 178 (1958); Spence & Zuo, *Electron Microdiffraction* (1992)
- **Status:** anchored
- **Checks:** Units are eV from e²/V. The sign is attractive. The two-node `g²` slope at `g = 0.05, 0.1 Å⁻¹` has `O(g²⟨r⁴⟩)` error of about 2e-3 relative (0.03 eV for Si). Si gives −13.80 eV, 0.8% from the independent-atom 13.91 V of Kruse et al. (2006) Table 2 (Doyle–Turner Hartree–Fock factors). Measured Si is 12.52 V (±0.71%, holography, same table), and DFT with bonding gives 12.57 V; the independent-atom value is 10% high, matching Kruse's attribution (7.5% bonding, 2.5% correlation). Diamond is deeper.
- **Anchor:** `tests/materials/test_channeling_entry.py::test_mean_inner_potential_matches_independent_atom_and_measured_si`
- **Notes:** Used only for entrance-face refraction (`cr-plane-incidence`). Transverse levels are referenced to `U_0 = 0` (`cr-planar-continuum-potential`). **2026-10-15 fresh-context verdict `rederived`** ([write-up](channeling/cr-mean-inner-potential.md)): the exact Waasmaier–Kirfel slope `Σ a_i b_i/16π²` gives Si −13.830 eV and diamond −20.816 eV, against the code's −13.799 and −20.790. The whole gap is the finite-difference error. Independence caveat: the verifier saw the function body after writing its formula. **Reference values** (Kruse, Schowalter, Lamoen, Rosenauer & Gerthsen, Ultramicroscopy 106, 105 (2006), Table 2, [doi:10.1016/j.ultramic.2005.06.057](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ultramic.2005.06.057)): Si independent-atom 13.91 V, DFT 12.57 V, holography 12.52 V; Ge 15.58 / 14.67 / 14.3(2) V (Ge not in the catalog). Gajdardziska-Josifovska et al., Ultramicroscopy 50, 285 (1993), Table 2, report Si 9.26(8) V; Kruse et al. note it is far below their DFT and holography values and it is not used as the anchor. The ~1.3 eV bonding error shifts the refraction only, which is negligible at MeV energies (`cr-plane-incidence`). Human sign-off pending.

## `cr-plane-incidence`

The transverse wavevector is `k_x = p_in·n`, and the effective incidence passed to `entry_populations` is `k_x/p`.
- **Code:** `materials/channeling/entry.py::plane_incidence`
- **Source:** reciprocal-lattice geometry (`surface-hkl-orientation`); electron refraction at a crystal surface by the mean inner potential (Spence & Zuo 1992)
- **Status:** anchored

- **Anchor:** `tests/materials/test_channeling_entry.py::{test_beam_direction_matches_transport_tilt_convention,test_tilt_across_versus_along_a_perpendicular_plane,test_refraction_conserves_tangential_momentum_and_raises_normal_momentum,test_miscut_tilts_channeling_planes_off_the_face_normal,test_catalog_face_orientation_is_default,test_cut_crystal_entry_feeds_populations}`

## `cr-lindblad-jump-operators`

- **Claim:** incoherent scattering enters the transverse density matrix as the dissipator `dρ/dz ⊃ [C(x,x′) − ½C(x,x) − ½C(x′,x′)] ρ(x,x′)`, with the kernel `C = Σ_k L_k L_k†` per unit depth and absorptive potential `W(x) = (ħv/2) C(x,x)`. **TDS** (Einstein model) is the per-atom phase covariance `C(x,x′) = (ħv)⁻² Σ_G n_G e^{iGx} ∫dp dq_y/(2π)² v(p+G,q_y) v(p,q_y) e^{ip(x−x′)} [e^{−a²G²/2} − e^{−a²((p+G)²+p²+2q_y²)/2}]`, with `v(q) = −4πe²(Z − f0(q))/q²`, static site densities `n_G` and `a² = B/8π²`. **Electronic** (local-density approximation, localization length `1/|q⊥|`): `C_el(x,x′) = ∫d²q⊥ w(q) √(ρ_q(x)ρ_q(x′)) e^{iq_x(x−x′)}`, where `ρ_q` is `ρ_e` Gaussian-smoothed with `σ = 1/|q⊥|` and `w` is the per-electron PENELOPE-2024 shell GOS resolved in `q⊥` (distant longitudinal `(pref f_k/W)/(π q²(1+Q/mc²))`; close Møller mapped by `(ħc q⊥)² = 2mc²W(1−W/E)`). The decoherence diagnostic is the cell average `Γ(Δ) = ⟨½C(x,x) + ½C(x+Δ,x+Δ) − Re C(x,x+Δ)⟩_x`.

## `cr-line-shape`

- **Claim:** the radiating coherences `σ_k = ⟨f_k|ρ|i_k⟩` of one transition `(i, f)` in all Bloch blocks `k` obey the secular equation `dσ/dz = K σ`, with `K_kk = iΔ_k/ħv − (Γ_i + Γ_f)_k/2 + J_kk` and `K_k′k = ⟨f_k′|J(|f_k⟩⟨i_k|)|i_k′⟩` (`Γ_n = ⟨n|C(x,x)|n⟩`; the kernel transfers coherence between blocks, since a momentum kick acting on ket and bra leaves no which-path information). By the quantum regression theorem the transverse spectrum is `S(ε) = Re Σ_m w̄_m (i/π)/(ε − ε_m + i h_m)`, with `K = V diag(μ) V⁻¹`, `ε_m = ħv Im μ_m`, `h_m = −ħv Re μ_m` and weights `w_m = (a†V)_m (V⁻¹ s)_m`, `s_k = Y_k/ā_k` from the line yields; `Σ w_m` is the total yield. For flat bands this is one Lorentzian of half width `ħv κ`, `κ_k = (Γ_i + Γ_f)_k/2 − Re Σ_k′ (ā_k′ a_k/|a_k|²) J_k′k` (reported as `hwhm_eV`). In the lab, centres and half widths are divided by `1 − β cos θ_e`. Each mode is convolved with a Gaussian of RMS `σ = |E_m| s σ_γ/γ`, `s = d ln E_c/d ln γ = cos θ_e/(γ²β(1 − β cos θ_e)) + d ln ΔE_⊥/d ln γ` (forward `(1 + β)/β + d ln ΔE/d ln γ`), giving complex Voigt (Faddeeva) profiles. The Doppler–geometric width comes from quadrature over the detector cone (Gauss–Legendre radius, uniform azimuth) and the in-plane beam divergence (Gauss–Hermite), each node with its own `θ_e`.
